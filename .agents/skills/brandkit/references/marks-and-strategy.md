# Marks and strategy

Use this reference when developing a new mark, rather than merely presenting a supplied logo.

Extract the brand's purpose, audience, personality, and concrete differentiator from the brief. A useful concept can be explained in one sentence. Ask only about missing choices that would materially change the identity; use context for reversible visual decisions.

## Concept families

| Family | Useful connection | Craft check |
|---|---|---|
| Monogram | Initials whose relationship expresses the brand | Reads at small size; avoids accidental letters |
| Product-derived | A distinctive action, tool, or physical property | Remains recognizable after simplification |
| Metaphor | A specific story such as connection, shelter, or precision | Explains this brand rather than an entire industry |
| Negative space | Two meanings carried by one shape | Both readings survive at practical sizes |
| Geometric system | Repeatable angles, arcs, cuts, or proportions | Consistent geometry across mark and applications |

Choose the family for its meaning, not at random. Explore alternatives when the user requests concepts or the direction remains uncertain; a settled brief does not require a fixed number of discarded options.

Keep the silhouette clear, optical balance intentional, and details few enough to survive reduction. Examine dark/light reversal and small-size behavior if those uses are in scope. Protect clear space and avoid mockup perspective disguising flaws in the actual mark.

Preserve exact spelling. Taglines are draft copy until approved and supported. Do not invent awards, founding dates, customers, security certifications, or performance claims as identity decoration. Deliver a generated image as an image concept; production vector construction, font licensing, and trademark research are separate deliverables unless requested.
