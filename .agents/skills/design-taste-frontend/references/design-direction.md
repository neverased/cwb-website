# Design direction

These are optional ways to reason about a brief, not mandatory scores or random selections.

| Dial | Restrained | Balanced | Expressive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design variance | Symmetry, predictable alignment | Varied sections and asymmetric emphasis | Editorial offsets, unusual image placement |
| Motion intensity | Static content and clear state changes | Brief purposeful transitions | Isolated cinematic sequences where requested |
| Visual density | Gallery spacing | Ordinary product UI | Compact data display with strong grouping |

Use content and user tasks to select density. A dashboard may favor tabular numbers and aligned rows; a portfolio may let the artifact lead. A reading surface needs comfortable measure and sustained hierarchy. A landing page needs a clear proposition and credible route to action. None requires a particular font family, card count, or theme.

## Pattern options

- Hero: editorial split, centered statement, product canvas, full-bleed image, quiet text-led introduction. Choose according to the real content and viewport; do not hide the main action below decorative spectacle.
- Grouping: rows and dividers, a consistent grid, asymmetric bento, grouped sections, or a gallery. Cards are useful for meaningful grouping or interaction, not only elevation.
- Navigation: ordinary header, sidebar, contextual tabs, or a compact floating treatment. Preserve familiar behavior and clear current location.
- Media: real product imagery, documentary photographs, diagrams, or an intentional illustration system. Typography alone can be the right choice; no image quota applies.
- Detail: typographic contrast, considered radii, subtle surface differences, or a small distinctive interaction. Repetition should establish a system, not create mechanical filler sections.

## Existing systems and redesigns

Read the actual tokens and representative components before selecting replacements. Respect project themes, supported locales, approved brand fonts, and platform conventions. A dark-only product need not gain a light theme. Keep established component libraries unless the requested change justifies replacing them.

For a redesign, identify concrete weaknesses such as unclear hierarchy, inaccessible contrast, crowded forms, confusing navigation, or irrelevant imagery. Fix those weaknesses; do not treat common fonts, centered headings, plain backgrounds, or three-column grids as defects by themselves.
