# Mobile visual system

Choose a visual direction from the product, audience, and existing identity. Keep it consistent across the requested screens. There is no required count of signature components, decorative layers, images, or motion cues.

## Composition and media

Use a dominant focus, supporting content, and a clear action. A dashboard may use aligned rows and tabular figures; a gallery can let media lead; an editorial app can use type and whitespace. Cards are useful for grouping, but avoid nested frames that waste phone width.

Use real supplied imagery where available. Generated illustration or a contextual photograph may help an image-led product; a form or utility screen can be entirely typographic. Keep text out of busy image regions or provide a contrast surface. Do not use generated portraits as purported real customers, staff, or endorsers.

Preserve meaningful image crops and subjects. A detail inspection can use zoom or a crop; actual image edits should follow the available tool's edit contract. Do not regenerate an entire screen when a small correction or clearer inspection suffices.

## Typography and color

Use platform-appropriate readable type, a coherent hierarchy, and enough line length for meaningful copy. Keep exact authorized wording where supplied. If small generated text is illegible, simplify or correct it; do not claim unreadable text is exact.

Pick one coherent palette, preserving approved brand colors. Reserve emphasis for priority and state; do not rely only on color for meaning. Textured backgrounds and expressive type are optional, never a requirement for ordinary product UI. Avoid unsupported contrast claims based solely on an image prompt.

For category inspiration, fitness can emphasize progress, finance clarity and trust, culture the work itself, and travel place and itinerary. These are design themes, not permission to invent health outcomes, balances, testimonials, bookings, or other factual content.
