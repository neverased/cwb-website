# Website art direction

Make the product, audience, and primary task visible. Choose a narrative idea from the brief, then express it through composition rather than adding generic decoration. Preserve supplied branding, copy, and verified product imagery.

## Composition options

Hero structures include a centered typographic statement, editorial split, product-led canvas, full-bleed documentary image, compact navigation-led introduction, or asymmetric gallery. Choose one suited to the content; none is forbidden by default. Let the hero's scale follow the message and viewport rather than requiring a giant headline or a fixed line count.

Vary section rhythm only when it helps explain different content: a quiet statement, a dense comparison, an image story, a clear action. Use rows, grids, lists, cards, or tables according to meaning. Avoid a fixed count of signature components, decorative elements, or motion indicators.

## Visual systems

- Editorial: considered type pairing, comfortable measure, selective imagery, generous hierarchy.
- Product or technical: clear information structure, precise alignment, real product detail, restrained accents.
- Cultural or experiential: artifact-led composition, strong art direction, distinctive but readable type.
- Minimal: flat surfaces, reduced palette, one clear focus, whitespace shaped by content.
- Cinematic: purposeful imagery, controlled contrast, depth that leaves copy and actions readable.

Use a consistent accent and neutral family when designing a new system, while preserving an established brand's palette. Do not treat purple, black, gradients, or familiar fonts as errors independent of context. Check that muted text and layered image backgrounds remain legible.

Keep the primary action clear and credible. Use only real or explicitly draft claims. Quotes and customer logos require supplied or verified support; invented social proof is not an art-direction technique.
