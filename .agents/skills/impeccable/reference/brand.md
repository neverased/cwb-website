# Brand and expressive surfaces

Use this guidance when the requested surface aims to explain, persuade, or let someone experience the work. The brief and existing identity govern the direction; category familiarity is not itself a defect.

Make the subject and intended action clear. Build visual character from the product's real story, content, audience, and assets. An expressive page can use unusual composition, distinctive typography, material, or purposeful interaction; it need not use every technique or reject a familiar effective pattern.

Choose hierarchy, density, media, and section rhythm deliberately. A strong opening should communicate the actual subject rather than only atmosphere. Give each section a purpose; do not add unsupported metrics, social proof, or filler copy to complete a landing-page formula.

Preserve supplied fonts, colors, and brand commitments. Verify font availability and licenses before sourcing replacements. Warm neutrals, black, gradients, ordinary grids, and minimal layouts remain valid when the direction calls for them.

Use genuine product or subject imagery where needed. Generate a missing asset only within scope. Do not invent customer quotes, affiliations, awards, certifications, names, or facts. Clearly label sample content used in a requested concept.

Keep essential content visible without motion. Use animation when it advances the story or clarifies interaction, with reduced-motion, keyboard/touch access, and appropriate performance checks. Static and restrained experiences are valid.

Check the actual render against the brief and the selected design, resolve material issues, and complete the requested artifact. Do not require candidate tournaments, image quotas, or repeated aesthetic passes after the result meets its purpose.
